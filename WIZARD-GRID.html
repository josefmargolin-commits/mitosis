Mitosis · design spec · rev 2

Wizard Grid

Two rooms stacked vertically with one camera between them. Wizards upstairs cast balls down into the arena below, and keep casting whether or not you're watching.

Rev 2 — the two-screen change fixes the one thing that was wrong

The previous draft squeezed the board above the arena and had to trade wizard size against arena size. Both surfaces lost. Splitting them into two rooms removes the trade entirely: full-size wizards, full-size arena.

It also turns out to make where a wizard stands mechanically meaningful rather than just tidy — see §5. That was a free win from your change, not something I'd designed for.

1 · What this replaces, and what it doesn't

The current producer is the gantry: DROPS[] heads sweeping a rail, fed by four upgrades. That whole assembly is what the wizards take over. Everything below the drop line is untouched.

Retired

DROPS[], drawGantry, headX, syncHeads, releaseBall, and the rate / volume / heads upgrades. All subsumed by per-wizard timers.

Retired

BALLS[] — the eight classes. Their taps / mass / size fields move onto the wizard rank table, which has the same shape.

Untouched

Fusion, splitting, rupture, detonation, combo, the metaball layer, powers, boosts, objectives, trails, palettes, scales, audio. The entire arena.

Kept, re-homed

cap and purity move to a new Arena tab alongside yield. magnet, chain, reson, fusion, attract stay in Field.

Still the one real collision: ball classes have to go

A ball has two axes today — tier (size, colour, fuse position) and class (taps, mass). The gantry always spawns at tier 3 and class is the only thing that grows.

Your wizards want to spawn different tiers. If classes stayed, a rank-5 wizard and a Singularity would be two ladders doing one job, and every purchase would sit on one of them arbitrarily.

So the wizard rank becomes the class. WRANK[r] exposes {taps, mass, size} — byte-compatible with what makeBall() already takes as its cls argument. That's why this change is cheap.

3 · Two rooms, one camera Q1 · Q15

The world is a vertical strip two viewports tall. The camera shows one at a time and slides between them.

↕
Arena

How it's built

The wizard room is DOM, not canvas. It's a grid of interactive elements with text, drag targets and panels — everything DOM is already good at and canvas is a chore for. The arena stays exactly as it is, three canvases untouched.

#world            // translateY(camY * 100%) — the camera
  .room--wizard   // DOM grid, plus one small canvas for spell bolts
  .room--arena    // #bg #goo #fx — completely unchanged
#topbar           // fixed above both; mass and spores always visible

The camera is one CSS transform on #world, so the slide is GPU-composited and costs nothing. camY runs 0 → −1.

Three consequences worth stating plainly
  • The simulation never pauses. step(dt) runs every frame regardless of which room you're looking at — wizard timers, ball physics, fusion, all of it. Only drawing is conditional.
  • The arena stops drawing when it's off-screen. The metaball filter is the single most expensive thing in the game, so the wizard room will run cooler than the arena does. During the slide, both rooms draw.
  • The bottom chrome is per-room. Arena keeps its tab dock; the wizard room gets a summon bar and a selected-wizard panel. The top bar is global, because you need to see your money while upgrading.

4 · Moving between them Q13

Target feel: a camera move, not a menu. 380 ms, cubic-bezier(.32,.72,.24,1) — quick off the mark, settling rather than bouncing.

  • Up arrow — top centre of the arena, below the currency readouts. Carries a dot when something upstairs wants attention: an affordable summon, or a legal merge sitting on the board.
  • Down arrow — bottom centre of the wizard room, above the summon bar.
  • Parallax — the wizard room's backdrop translates at ~0.85× the foreground, so the rooms feel like they're at different depths rather than a flat sheet sliding.
  • Audio — an ascending filtered whoosh going up, descending coming down. Haptic tick on arrival.
  • Seam — a soft shadow under the wizard room's floor edge, so the arena reads as genuinely below it.
Gesture conflict — this one would have bitten us

Swipe is already the arena's core verb: pointermove cleaves every ball the line crosses. A swipe-up-to-ascend gesture would fire constantly during normal play.

So: on the arena screen, the arrow is the only way up. No swipe. In the wizard room there's no such conflict, so swipe-down works anywhere that isn't a wizard being dragged, alongside the arrow.

Asymmetric, but the alternative is a game that cleaves your board every time you try to leave it.

5 · Columns are a real decision Q2

A wizard in column c drops its balls at x = (c + 0.5) / 4 × W, jittered a few pixels. Four columns, four entry lanes.

This makes rearranging the board strategic, not cosmetic

Balls only fuse when two of the same colour touch and hold for 0.42 s. Two same-rank wizards in the same column drop their balls into the same lane — they land near each other and fuse almost immediately. Spread across different columns, they arrive far apart and mostly don't.

So stacking a column is a fusion engine, and spreading is a breadth strategy that keeps the arena evenly fed and easier to work by hand. Neither is strictly better, and the player discovers it by watching rather than by reading a tooltip.

In rev 1 "move wizards around the grid" was housekeeping. Now it's a build.

Row position has no mechanical effect. I'd leave it that way — one spatial axis carrying meaning is legible, two is a puzzle.

6 · Casting, from both sides Q14

One event, two presentations, and the mechanical part is identical either way.

Seen from the arena

The ball materialises just above the top edge in its wizard's column, with the existing muzzle burst — ring, sparks, pop() pitched to the tier, panned by x — then falls under normal physics. Effectively what the gantry does now, minus the machine.

Seen from the wizard room

  1. Charge — last 0.4 s of the timer: staff-tip glows, the cell's progress ring closes, the figure crouches.
  2. Release — snaps upright with recoil, a glyph flashes, and a bolt of the ball's colour launches downward.
  3. Exit — the bolt accelerates and disappears off the bottom edge of the screen. You watch your production leave for the room below.

Drawn on a small dedicated canvas layered over the wizard grid, so bolts pass in front of the wizards and behind the panels.

Blocked casts

If the arena is at capacity the wizard holds at full charge and visibly strains — glow pulsing, no release, a small warning pip on the cell. From the arena you see nothing arriving; from upstairs you see exactly why. Alongside that, the wizard room shows a live arena strip — a capacity bar and ball count — so you can tell from upstairs when the dish needs you.

7 · Merging Q3 · Q9

Drag a wizard onto another of the same rank. Both vanish into a swirl; the successor rises from the target slot with a flash, a shake, and a chime one step up the pentatonic ladder. Tap-select then tap-target does the same, with legal targets outlined while a wizard is held.

What happens to upgrades — the decision that sets the strategy
  • Lose everything. Nobody upgrades anything they might merge. Dead end.
  • Sum the levels. Compounds violently; one super-wizard becomes the only play.
  • Refund and reset. Clean, but every merge feels like a step backwards.
  • Inherit the higher level, refund half the lower one. ← recommended

The last gives a strategy you can say in one line: pick a champion, pour upgrades in, feed it fodder. The champion keeps everything you spent; the fodder returns half. Nothing is ever wasted, and choosing which slot becomes the champion is a real decision — especially now that the column it stands in matters.

8 · Merge economics Q5 · Q10

Two rank-r wizards use two slots and produce 2 × T(r). One rank-r+1 uses one slot and produces 2.7 × T(r). A merge is therefore:

  • ×1.35 throughput per slot — real, but small
  • −1 slot used — the actual prize, once the board is full

Early, with empty slots, summoning two Apprentices beats merging. Late, with sixteen full, merging is the only direction left. The crossover arrives on its own as the board fills; no rule has to announce it. A fully-upgraded wizard is ×3.4, more than a merge gives, so upgrading is a genuine third option.

Pacing target

World 1 completes at 100 K banked. From one Apprentice at ~7 mass/sec, the run has to average ~200/sec to land in an 8–12 minute window. That means reaching roughly rank 2–3 plus a handful of upgrades — the intended shape of a first world.

How I'd verify it

Not by feel. The economy is a closed system, so I'd write a headless simulator that plays it under three policies — merge-greedy, upgrade-greedy, mixed — and reports time-to-100K for each. Within ~25% of each other means the strategy space is real. If one dominates by 3×, the numbers are wrong, and I'd rather find that in a script than in your hands.

9 · Upgrades and prices Q4 · Q6 · Q7

Upgrades attach to the wizard instance, never to the rank. Three Magi can sit on the board at levels 14, 0 and 0.

// speed: the primary upgrade, and the only one I'd ship in v1
delay(w)  = WRANK[w.rank].delay * 0.94 ** w.lvl     // floors at 0.30 × base, ~lvl 20
upCost(w) = 90 * 7 ** w.rank * 1.19 ** w.lvl

The 7 ** rank term is what makes rank matter: an Apprentice's first upgrade is 90, an Ascendant's is 1.5 M. Cheap wizards are cheap to tune; expensive ones are a commitment.

Two secondaries held back for later — Potency (mass multiplier) and Surge (chance to cast twice). Three sliders per wizard on day one is too many.

Summoning

Everything above rank 0 comes from merging. Rank 0 is bought into any empty slot:

summonCost = 120 * 1.42 ** summonsThisWorld

First 120, eighth 1.9 K, sixteenth 30 K — roughly 90 K to fill an empty board against a 100 K world goal. You can't buy a full board and finish the world. You have to merge.

10 · Keeping it a game Q12

The risk is that automated production turns the arena into a screensaver. Four levers, and I'd want all four:

Wizards produce, you realise

A ball is worth nothing until broken down to the smallest rung. Wizards create potential; only tapping converts it.

Lysis gets demoted

The auto-splitter becomes an offline trickle rather than competitive income, so active play is always strictly better.

A full arena stalls the board

Wizards hold their charge when the dish is full. Neglect downstairs and production visibly stops upstairs.

Tap to force a cast

Tapping a charged wizard fires it early and resets its timer. Working both rooms beats idling in either.

The two-room split sharpens this rather than softening it: being upstairs has a real cost, because nobody is clearing the dish while you shop.

12 · Interaction Q13

  • Tap empty slot → summon, live price shown. Disabled rather than hidden below cost, so you can see what you're saving for.
  • Tap wizard → panel docks at the bottom of the room: rank, ball it makes, current delay, next-level delta, upgrade button with cost. The board stays visible above it.
  • Hold + drag → lifts the wizard; same-rank targets outline for merge, empty slots dim-outline for a move.
  • Tap-select → tap-target → identical outcomes, for anyone who finds drag fiddly on glass.
  • Long-press → stats tooltip, no state change.
  • Down arrow / swipe down → back to the arena.

Rearranging is always free. It costs nothing mechanically, people want a tidy board anyway, and now it also decides which lane each wizard feeds.

Merge, precisely

merge(from, to):
  a = G.wiz[from]; b = G.wiz[to]
  require a.rank === b.rank && a.rank < WRANK.length - 1

  keep   = Math.max(a.lvl, b.lvl)
  loser  = a.lvl <= b.lvl ? a : b
  refund = Math.floor(loser.spent * 0.5)

  G.wiz[to]   = { id:++G.wizSeq, rank:a.rank + 1, lvl:keep,
                  spent: Math.max(a.spent, b.spent) }
  G.wiz[from] = null
  G.bio      += refund

14 · Build order

Sequenced so the game is playable at the end of every phase, rather than broken for the duration.

1
Rank table, no UI

Add WRANK, point the existing gantry at it instead of BALLS, retire the classes and the Balls tab. The game plays exactly as it does now — this phase only proves the substitution is really byte-compatible.

2
The camera

#world wrapper, both rooms, up and down arrows, the 380 ms slide, per-room chrome. The wizard room is still empty scaffolding. Nothing about the arena changes except that it now lives inside a container.

3
Wizards that cast

G.wiz, the 4×4 render, per-wizard timers, castBall(), column-to-lane mapping. Delete the gantry. One starter Apprentice, no summoning or merging yet — just watch a wizard feed the dish from upstairs.

4
The loop

Summon prices, drag and tap-target merging, the upgrade panel, refunds, the live arena strip, held-and-straining state. This is where it becomes a game rather than a demo.

5
Balance

Headless simulator, three policies, tune until nothing dominates. Rewire world reset. Demote Lysis. Repoint offline earnings at wizard throughput.

6
Feel

Cast animation and falling bolts, merge burst, charge glow, parallax and seam shadow, transition audio, per-world caster art and room backdrops, tap-to-force-cast.

15 · What I still need from you

✓ Settled — the board is its own screen

Your call, and it's the right one. Full-size wizards, full-size arena, and column position gained a mechanical role it didn't have.

1 · Do the eight ball classes go?

Still the one blocking decision. My recommendation is yes — the wizard rank does their job, and two ladders for one idea reads as noise. But the Balls tab is something you asked for, so it's your call.

2 · Inherit-the-higher-level on merge?

It makes "build a champion, feed it fodder" the core strategy. Legible and satisfying, but it is a strategy rather than an open field — worth confirming that's the game you want.

3 · Six ranks, or more?

Six means 32 Apprentices for one Ascendant — a full world's chase. Eight would need a taller ball ladder than the arena currently has, so it isn't free.

4 · Should columns matter?

New question, from your change. Column-to-lane mapping makes board layout a build rather than housekeeping — I think it's the best thing to come out of the two-screen idea. But it does mean a tidy board and an effective board are different things, which some players find annoying rather than interesting.
